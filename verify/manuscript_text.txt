From Iss to I-distance: Quantifying DNA Evolution with Information Theory 
 
Zhi-Jie Xu 
College of Biological Science & Medical Engineering, Donghua University, Shanghai 
201620, People’s Republic of China. e-mail. zjxmolls@mail.dhu.edu.cn 
 
Abstract. The DNA evolutionary process is traditionally inferred by evaluating certain 
substitution models for homologous sequences, which is often computationally intensive and 
black-box. Here, we report that the Information-based index of substitution saturation (Iss), 
conventionally used as a mere index for substitution saturation diagnosis, in fact is a universal 
informational-theoretic ruler for genetic divergence. Through extensive evolutionary 
simulations, we demonstrate that Iss exhibits a precise, model-dependent relationship with p-
distance, effectively decoding the hidden evolutionary signature embedded within sequence 
alignments. Consequently, we repurpose Iss from a saturation watchdog into a powerful and 
model-agnostic statistics and purpose for a new name, informational distance (abbr. i-distance). 
Furthermore, we show that a combined use of i-distance and traditional statistics can directly 
distinguish evolutionary models (e.g., JC69, K2P, GTR), diagnose topology biases, and even 
signal the presence of mixed evolutionary processes. This discovery bridges information theory, 
statistics, and molecular phylogenetics, laying the foundation for a new paradigm, 
Informational Molecular Evolution, that allows for the visual and quantitative dissection of 
evolutionary forces directly from sequence data. 
 
Keywords. Molecular phylogenetics, molecular evolution, information theory, informational 
distance, evolutionary simulation, substitution saturation. 
 
Introduction 
Unraveling the history and mechanisms of molecular evolution relies on comparing 
homologous sequence alignments. In the past 60 years’ development of computational 
molecular evolution, the only path of this effort is the selection of an appropriate substitution 
model, which is inherently a statistical model-fitting problem. Current approaches, based on 
likelihood maximization and information criteria, or the Markov Chain Monte Carlo, while 
powerful, operate as "black boxes". While outputting a best-fit model, they offer limited 
intuitive insight into why that model best fits or how the evolutionary process visibly manifests 
in the data. Moreover, the analysis of saturated sequences remains a big challenge, as standard 
genetic distance estimates become indeterminate or completely invalid facing saturated 
alignments. 
The Index of substitution saturation (Iss) statistic, introduced by Xia et al.[1, 2] in 2003, was 
designed to address the latter problem. It measures the degree of saturation by comparing the 
average informational entropy of observed substitutions (Hm) to the maximum possible 
entropy of the observed base (Hfss). For over two decades, its utility has only been confined to 
a simple decision: whether sequences are too saturated for reliable phylogenetic inference. The 
critical value for this decision (Iss.c) is derived from complex simulations, making the function 
hard to reproduce and underlying richness of Iss overlooked. 

=== PAGE 2 ===
In this work, we demonstrate that this conventional view represents a profound underutilization 
of the Iss statistic. We show that Iss is not merely a gatekeeper but a versatile informational-
theoretic descriptor of sequence polymorphism relevant to multiple variables of the substitution 
process. This redefinition unlocks an entirely new suite of its applications, transforming Iss 
into a powerful and intuitive tool for diagnosing multiple perspectives of the very evolutionary 
processes that traditional methods seek to model. 
 
Results 
 
1. Establishing the Fundamental Behavior of Iss 
We began by systematically replicating and extending the original framework for calculating 
Iss.c. Large-scale simulations were automatically completed under various models (JC69, K2P, 
F81, F84, HKY85, TN93, GTR), model rate and base frequency parameters, gamma 
distribution parameters, tree topologies, and branch lengths, with their Iss values automatically 
calculated. We noticed that Iss can be influenced by all variables in our simulations, and 
monotonically increases to tree lengths (Figure 1), and an amazing pattern emerged: Under 
same tree topologies and other model parameters, the median of Iss distribution is irrelevant to 
sequence lengths, but increases with the growth of tree lengths, while its deviation narrows 
with the increase of sequence lengths (Figure 2A). What is even better, across all models, Iss 
formed a smooth, continuous curve when plotted against the mean p-distance of the 4-sequence 
set (Figure 2B). This robust, monotonic relationship revealed that Iss is a precise proxy for 
genetic divergence, a role far exceeding its original binary saturation-detection purpose. 
When in 2-sequence case, each site’s entropy is restricted to zero (same bases) or one (divergent 
bases), indicating the degeneration of Hm to the common pairwise p-distance (Figure 3B), 
while the base-frequency information included in Hfss makes the scatter points of Iss rising a 
bit when base frequencies are unequal (Figure 3A), despite its general strong correlation to 
pairwise p-distance. This is also observed in our simulation. 
It is worth noting that by definition, Iss is lower than one in an ideal situation, but its systematic 
variation, as deducted by Xia’s work, may let it be beyond this limit when being observed. 
 
2. Unveiling the Dual Nature of Iss: An Informational-Theoretic Measure of DNA 
polymorphism 
We discovered that the point of maximum saturation (Iss = 1) corresponds precisely to the 
theoretical maximum p-distance under a given model (e.g., ~0.75 for JC69), which can also be 
deducted with the Markov chain steady states of base frequencies. This critical finding bridges 
the informational-theoretic definition of Iss with the statistical concept of p-distance and the 
steady-state of nucleotide substitution Markov processes. It reveals that Iss encapsulates the 
same core information—sequence polymorphism—as p-distance, but through a model-latent 
and informational-theoretic lens. This dual nature explains its fundamental behavior and 
provides a theoretical anchor for its application. 
 
3. Decoding the Hidden Layer: Iss as a Reservoir of Evolutionary Information and 
Model-Specific Signatures 
Having established Iss's model-decoding capacity, we now redefine it as Informational distance

(i-distance), a term better fitting its role as an evolutionary divergence measure. We 
hypothesized that if i-distance captures divergence, its relationship with p-distance might be 
shaped by the underlying evolutionary model. We decomposed the p-distance into its 
constituent components (e.g., transition vs. transversion p-distance for K2P; all six 
substitution-type distances for more complex models, e.g. GTR). Plotting i-distance against 
these components revealed model-specific fingerprints: nearly identical trajectories for JC69, 
two distinct clusters for K2P, and six unique trajectories for an unconstrained GTR model 
(Figure 4). 
 
Furthermore, Xia’s work revealed that Iss.c is sensitive to tree topology, with asymmetric 
topologies having lower Iss.c value than symmetric ones, indicating that Iss itself may be 
impacted by tree topology. Our work further proved that asymmetric trees yield systematically 
higher Iss values than symmetric trees of equal total length, revealing a previously hidden 
signature of evolutionary history. 
 
Discussion 
We have demonstrated that the Iss statistic is a rich, information-theoretic measure of 
evolutionary divergence, not a simple threshold. This redefinition transforms it from a simple 
tool into a core diagnostic for molecular evolution with great empirical potential. The ability 
to visually distinguish between evolutionary models and to detect heterogeneity directly from 
Iss-p plots indicates a paradigm shift from only black-box model-fitting to its collaboration 
with transparent model-diagnosis. Also, we propose renaming the Iss statistic as ‘Informational 
distance’ (i-distance) to better reflect its role as an information-theoretical measure of DNA 
polymorphism and sequence divergence, corresponding to proportional distance (p-distance). 
This work bridges the fields of information theory, statistics, and evolutionary biology. The Iss 
statistic acts as a translator, converting the statistical patterns of sequence alignment into 
informational terms, which we show are exquisitely sensitive to the parameters and processes 
of evolution. 
We propose that these findings establish the foundation for a new sub-discipline: Informational 
Molecular Evolution. This framework leverages information-theoretic[3] quantities to directly 
visualize, quantify, and diagnose evolutionary forces. Future applications are vast, including 
illuminating topological biases, resolving recombination and hybrids, analyzing saturated 
sequences, and developing information-based distance metrics immune to overflow. By 
looking at sequences through this new perspective, we gain an intuitive, powerful, and 
fundamentally different way to understand the molecular evolutionary process. 
We argue that this new paradigm must be bipedal, supported by its left and right arms: the basic 
statistics of information theory and the generative power of evolutionary simulation. In an era 
of abundant computing power, the path forward can be fully empirical: simulate under 
hypothesis, and compare the informational statistics of simulated outcomes to real data. This 
is the core of a powerful new hypothesis-testing framework. By looking at sequences through 
this informational lens, we gain an intuitive and profoundly different way to understand 
molecular evolution. 
 
Methods 

=== PAGE 4 ===
Large-scale simulations were performed using Evolver in PAML v4.10.7[4] to generate 
sequence alignments under multiple models (JC69, K2P, HKY, F81, F84, TN93, GTR[5]), 
gamma distributions, tree topologies (symmetric or asymmetric), and branch lengths, with the 
Iss statistic and p-distances calculated according to the definition in Xia et al. in a custom 
Python pipeline. All analysis code, simulation scripts, and plotting routines are available on 
Github. The heatmap was generated with matplotlib’s imshow function, and other plots in this 
work were generated with plotnine in Python.  
 
Data Avaliability 
All source codes of python scripts are available on the Github depository: 
https://github.com/Gipsy-The-Sheller/Iss-simulation-scripts. 
The TAR packages of all simulation results , vector graphs of individual plots (in SVG format) 
and partial corresponding bitmaps (in PNG format) are available on Zenodo via DOI: 
https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.17071721.
