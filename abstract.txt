Revisiting Xia's Index of Substitution Saturation from a Mathematical Perspective

Zhi-Jie Xu
College of Biological Science & Medical Engineering, Donghua University, Shanghai 201620, China

Abstract

The index of substitution saturation Iss = Hbar/HFSS (Xia et al. 2003) decides, before any tree is built, whether an alignment retains phylogenetic signal. The entropy enters E[Iss] only through the spectrum of the rate matrix: the index is a linear functional of the coordinates <e^(mu d)>, collapsing onto mean pairwise p-distance exactly when each non-zero eigenvalue's multiplicity-to-composition-weight ratio is constant across eigenvalues. This holds for every reversible model at uniform composition and for degenerate spectra; it fails for non-uniform composition with a non-degenerate spectrum, the regime it was calibrated in. To second order -- the order at which the entropy enters -- the index adds nothing under the collapse about the number of substitutions realised beyond the uncorrected proportion of differing sites; outside it, what it adds is the model's composition, which its user has not fixed. The mean of lambda reduces the same way.

The critical value fails separately: it carries no rate-heterogeneity coordinate, and near saturation the index is pinned within a few percent of its ceiling, a fifteen-fold change in the tree length at which recovery fails moving it by 1.2%. A four-taxon transfer of long branches at fixed patristic total makes this direct: the index is constant along the path while recovery spans its full range.

Real data occupy both: the saturated codon partition of the index's own data set falls between two readings of its threshold, and a genome-scale avian alignment sits five to six times below both. The index is not information-free -- in a crossed simulation it predicts recovery where p-distance does not -- but what it adds tracks the tree's shape and the higher-order composition of sites, not the substitution count; its threshold is a power threshold carrying its own sequence length. Passing the test is evidence against saturation, failing it is weak evidence, neither is evidence of resolution.
